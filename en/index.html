AI agent writing posts for a print shop

Live in production

a family-run print shop and graphic studio in Prague

A social media automation that runs non-stop on Railway. Every Sunday morning it finds topics, writes draft posts and saves them to Notion, where a person reads and approves them. Nothing gets published on its own.

  1. Sunday 7:00finds topics
  2. 8:00writes drafts
  3. Two checksfacts and Czech language
  4. Notiona person approves

Every number in a draft must have a source in a bank of verified facts about the company. A draft with an unsourced number gets one rewrite from the agent; if it still fails, it goes to Notion flagged for verification. When a made-up colleague once appeared in a draft, I traced the cause to the sample text the model had copied her from and fixed the rules, not just that one post.

The agent is built for multiple brands. The second one is the detailing business from Social media for the print shop and detailing.

Ťuk: a home log you tap with your phone

Live, in use at home

my own app for our household (“ťuk” is Czech for “tap”)

A home log you write to by tapping your phone. There are NFC stickers by the door, the plants and the bin. Tap an iPhone against one and the log records that the door is locked, the plants are watered or the bin is out, without opening the app. If something gets forgotten, a reminder arrives.

  1. Stickerby the door, the plants, the bin
  2. iPhone Shortcutruns on a tap
  3. Serververifies and records
  4. Logshared by the household

The idea came from home. We kept going back to the door to check whether it was locked, and nobody knew who had last watered the plants. Now we just open the log.

The log also shows who was home when, and when the flat is empty. That’s why I tackled security first: 33 documented risks, each with a mitigation, and an automated test that verifies one household can’t reach another household’s data.

Social media for the print shop and detailing

Launching now

a print shop and a mobile car detailing business

Posts and videos for social media. I’ve been preparing the groundwork since early August, and the first posts are going out now. I don’t make individual posts; I build the system that produces them: templates, verified facts, the brand’s voice and a check before anything is published.

It builds on the content agent. The agent drafts copy for the print shop. Finished posts with graphics, and videos for both brands, are made here from the brand voice and the verified facts.

The print shop’s content plan is based on an analysis of 300 of its posts from 2018 to 2026. Posts built around a photo performed better than pure graphics, so the new plan leans more on photos.

Claude Code edits the videos: batches of vertical videos through my own script, motion graphics in HyperFrames, and timeline edits in DaVinci Resolve, which it controls through MCP. I only approve the result.

3D models for jewellery and 3D printing

Client work

jewellery for casting, promotional items, animations

Blender controlled from Claude Code through MCP. It’s where rings and pendants for casting in silver, promotional items for 3D printing and social media animations are made. Every model ends up as a file for the goldsmith or the printer, plus renders for the client.

Before delivery, every model is measured, not just eyeballed: wall thickness, dimensions and weight, which sets the price of the silver. Mistakes are caught on screen, not in production.

The ring is a parametric script. A different size means changing one number, not an hour of modelling.

Blender via MCP · parametric Python scripts · STL for casting and 3D printing · renders and a 360° turntable

Construction schedules and cost analyses

Delivered · paid client work

construction companies

The client gets an Excel workbook and a print-ready PDF with dates for every activity. The cost analyses answer what the flats in a given location will sell for, what the construction will cost and what the gross profit will be.

  1. Activitiesand their dependencies
  2. Pythonbuilds the workbook with formulas
  3. Checkrecalculates the dates
  4. Excel and PDFfor the client

The workbook isn’t made by hand. Claude Code turns the client’s brief into activities and their dependencies, and a script builds the workbook with formulas from them. Python independently recalculates the dates from the formulas and compares both versions. One main script runs everything, from the data to the finished PDF.

I wrote the whole process down as a master prompt, a skill for Claude Code. It takes a job from the client’s email through source documents and assumptions to the schedule, the cost analysis, a cover note and the package to send. The next building is mostly new data, not new work.

Hermes Agent

Running via Telegram

an assistant for when I’m away from my computer

From my phone I send it a link or a topic over Telegram. For a forwarded social media post, it judges whether it fits my projects and records the verdict and the next step. Longer research and browsing of websites and social media can run overnight when needed.

  1. Link from my phonevia Telegram
  2. Hermesevaluates or researches
  3. Claude Codechecks the output in the morning
  4. Workspaceonly what passed

Hermes runs on a different language model than Claude. Every morning Claude Code checks its output and lets only what passes into the workspace. The author and the reviewer really are two different models.

The agent may only write to a dedicated folder and treats text from the web as data, not as instructions. I have to approve every command it runs on my computer.

SVAČINA: a game from a single prompt

Submitted to the contest

Summer Coding Challenge 2026 by profiq

The contest didn’t take code; it took a brief for an AI agent. From that brief, the agent had to build the game on its own, in a single run, within an hour and without any human intervention. The result is Snake turned inside out: you’re the apple, running from the snake across a squared notebook page.

  1. Briefup to 10,000 characters
  2. 25 test runs17 versions of the brief
  3. Freezeonly after three good runs in a row
  4. Submitted run51 minutes, no edits

There was no way to touch the result by hand, so all the work was in how to brief the agent. The submitted run met all 12 criteria, and the agent verified the game itself with automated checks.

How I work

Claude Code is my main tool. It runs every day on my computer and in the cloud, connected to GitHub, Supabase, Railway, Vercel and Notion. My job is to decide what gets built, specify it well and verify that the result does what it should. To keep that from being a lottery every time, I rely on three things.

The PACT workspace

A single repository where Claude Code has everything it needs. A new session doesn’t start from zero: Claude Code knows who I am, how I write, who I work for and what’s currently in progress.

  • PProjectseach has exactly one home in the registry
  • AAgents and skillsrepeated work as ready-made instructions
  • CContextme, my style, goals, clients and their brands
  • TToolsscripts and connections to services

Order is kept by a program, not by memory: a workspace check reports forgotten branches, projects missing from the registry and broken links.

Every Sunday a review runs that cleans up duplicates and looks for manual work worth turning into a skill or an automation.

The process for every new project

  1. Idea filewhat and why
  2. Specificationroles and scope
  3. Risksthreat model
  4. Planstep by step
  5. Buildto the plan
  6. Testsautomated

The order is mandatory, and nothing gets built without documented risks. Documentation lives in the repository right next to the code: specification, risks, architecture, deployment from scratch and a decision log. Any project can be handed over at any time, for example to IT or a security team.

I’ve also written the process up for beginners so that it works in any AI tool, not just Claude Code.

Safeguards that run on their own

Some are enforced by hooks, others by rules the agent follows without being reminded.

  • I’m starting a new project.A hook reminds me of the mandatory process and the documentation checklist before the first line of code is written.
  • I’m installing a skill or a plugin.Before it installs, it goes through a security scanner from NVIDIA. If the scanner finds dangerous code, the installation stops.
  • I’m pushing changes to GitHub.A pre-push check blocks anything that looks like a key or a password file, and rejects changes whose tests fail.
  • Something is leaving my computer.An output gate: an automated language check, a read-through by eye, fact-checking against the source, and the whole thing again after the last edit. This portfolio went through it too.
  • Someone other than the author reviews it.A draft post is checked by a different step than the one that wrote it. The same goes for the dates in a schedule, a 3D model for the goldsmith or the output from Hermes.
